Advocacy

Become Involved Through Advocacy

Step 1

Take Measures To Begin Advocacy

Take early measures to begin advocacy and contact the FLOTE parent organization for further assistance.
Examples of early steps you can take to begin advocacy include:

  • Reaching out to local representatives and elected officials regarding issues facing the Tamil community.
  • Starting petitions demanding positive change in the community.
  • Partner with existing advocacy organizations in the community to strengthen the cause.
Step 2

Training For Advocacy

FLOTE is committed to educating and empowering young Tamils to make a difference in their communities. Our plan includes virtual sessions to train members in effective outreach strategies.

Step 3

In The Classroom

Textbooks and school lessons often misrepresent Tamil history. FLOTE emphasizes the need for amiable, facts-based dialogue to advocate for change. History curriculums sometimes all together ignore Tamil history. FLOTE plans to organize members to take friendly, fact-based actions to engage in dialogue for the inclusion of Tamil history into curriculums.

Step 4

In Local and State Government

Government campaigns typically revolve around recognizing the Tamil genocide in Sri Lanka. FLOTE strives for a grassroots movement of municipalities and states recognizing the Tamil genocide in an effort to bring national and international attention to the issue.

View or Download: Letter to Elected Official
Step 5

In Congress

FLOTE encourages members to communicate with their representatives in Congress through emails, phone calls, and letters. Our mission is to build awareness of human rights abuses in Sri Lanka against Tamil people and to support democracy and self-determination in the area. The United States will be a crucial ally to the Tamil people in standing up to a China-backed Sri Lankan government.
